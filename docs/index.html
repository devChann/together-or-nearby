Together 🤝
or just nearby?

Two phones at the same place at the same time don't make two friends hanging out. Colleagues share an office every weekday. Strangers share a canteen. So for every encounter this asks what a friends map needs to know: was either of them there by routine, did they turn up and leave together, and is that spot usually busy at that hour?

    – 🤝 together press play ✨

    🧪 Does it work?

    Nobody labelled which GeoLife encounters were meetups, so the test comes from the data itself. Shift one person of every pair by whole weeks: their weekly routines still line up, but any plan to meet doesn't. Every encounter between these fake pairs is coincidence by construction. The rules were designed against a 5-week shift and scored once against a 9-week shift that was kept back until the end.

    🛠️ How it's built

    A reproducible pipeline: raw GeoLife files to Parquet with DuckDB, then dbt models (SQL plus two Python models) with data tests, then this static page. The whole run, from raw files to this page, takes about four minutes on a laptop.

          In short: on GeoLife GPS data (171 people, Beijing 2007–2012), this detector calls 41% of real encounters meetups and 1.7% of encounters between fake pairs in a held-back test. Along the way it found 1.55 million GPS fixes copied between user IDs. Built with DuckDB, dbt, H3 and MapLibre.
